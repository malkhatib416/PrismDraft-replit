# PrismDraft: product brief

You are building PrismDraft. This describes what it is and what has to be true
about it. The architecture, language, framework and datastore are yours to
choose: decide, say why in one paragraph, then build.

## What it is

A content workspace that turns a topic into a publication-ready article with
images that belong to it. A team briefs it, reviews what comes back, approves it,
and publishes to the CMS they already run.

It is not a chat window with a copy button. The unit of work is an article moving
through a team, not a message.

## Who it is for

Content and SEO teams of roughly 3 to 30 people who publish on a schedule and
answer to somebody for what goes out: agencies, SaaS companies, publishers,
affiliate operations. A writer drafts, an editor decides, and a third person has
the keys to production. The product exists because that division of labour is
real, and most AI writing tools pretend it is not.

## The loop

A workspace holds projects. A project holds a brand voice, a knowledge base and
its own articles. Inside a project someone writes a brief, gets an outline they
can edit, approves it, and the article is written against it. Images are
generated for it. A reviewer reads it, asks for changes or approves it. Someone
with the right permission publishes it. Everything that happened is on the
record.

## What makes it worth paying for

1. **It plans before it writes.** The outline comes first as editable structure,
   and the prose is written against the version the human approved.
2. **Images read the paragraph they sit beside.** Not the title, not a stock
   search of the topic.
3. **Nothing publishes itself.** Approval is a real gate, held by a real person.
4. **You can watch it and stop it.** A run in progress is visible and cancellable,
   and its progress survives a reload.
5. **You pay for what you actually used**, and you see the estimate first.

## Non-negotiables

These are behaviours, not implementation choices. Break one and it is a different
product.

- **It does not make things up.** No invented statistics, no fabricated sources,
  no search volumes without a real data provider, no claim that research or fact
  checking ran when it did not. When it lacks something, it says so or omits the
  claim. Skip, never simulate.
- **Approval is enforced, not suggested.** Only an approved article can be
  published. A rejection carries a comment or it does not happen. Every decision
  records who, what changed, and both states.
- **Tenants cannot see each other.** Not through an id in a URL, not through a
  search, not through an export, not through a webhook. Assume every id in a
  request is hostile.
- **Roles are composed, not chosen from a list.** Permissions are data. A person
  can hold several roles. Access inside a project may narrow what a role grants,
  never widen it. "Can generate but cannot publish" must be expressible without a
  code change.
- **Money is careful.** Credits are prepaid. The user sees an estimate, the cost
  is reserved before the work starts, the real cost is charged when it finishes,
  and the remainder goes back. Work that produced nothing charges nothing, and the
  rule for partial work is written down where a customer can read it. The record
  of charges is append-only. A payment confirmation that arrives twice credits
  once. Credit only when the payment provider says so, never because a browser
  reached a success page.
- **Expensive work is safely repeatable.** The same request twice yields one
  article, one charge, one notification.
- **The article is structured data**, not a blob of HTML. Every export and every
  CMS payload derives from it. Edits accumulate as versions, and restoring an old
  one adds a version rather than erasing history.
- **Everything consequential is traceable**: who did it, to what, when, and from
  where. Append-only.
- **A URL a user supplies is hostile** until proven otherwise. Source URLs,
  research targets, publishing endpoints and outbound webhooks all get checked
  before anything is fetched.

## Scope

**In for v1:** accounts and workspaces, composable roles, projects, brand voice,
briefs, outlines, generation with progress, contextual images, review and
approval, publishing to at least one real CMS, prepaid credits with a real
payment provider, usage visibility, an API with keys, and outbound webhooks.

**Out for v1, and say so in the UI rather than faking it:** web research, fact
checking, retrieval over uploaded documents, plagiarism and AI detection, SEO
analytics, content performance, automated internal linking.

## Done when

One person can sign up, create a workspace, invite a colleague on a narrower
role, buy credits, brief an article, watch it generate, disagree with it, get it
revised, approve it, publish it to a real CMS, and then see exactly what it cost
and who did what. A second workspace can see none of it. And every claim the
interface makes is true at runtime, not just in the copy.

## How to work

Build the thin end-to-end slice first, all the way from sign-up to published, and
only widen it once that holds. Prefer refusing to guessing, in the product and in
your own work: if something cannot be done honestly yet, leave it out and say
why.
